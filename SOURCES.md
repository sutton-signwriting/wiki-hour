# Sources for the Wiki Hour materials

Sources checked during preparation on 8–9 October 2026. These references support
the writing model, development tools, historical context, and proposed hosting route.

## Writing and history

- [What is SignWriting?](https://www.signwriting.org/about/what/what02.html): Valerie Sutton's introduction to the script.
- [Symposium Presentation 0097](https://www.signwriting.org/symposium/presentation0097.html): Val's answers to seven history questions, with interpreted material.
- [WFD/WASLI International Sign FAQ](https://wfdeaf.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/FAQ-on-IS-7-Nov-2019-Updated-Version-included-IS.docx.pdf): the distinction between International Sign and national signed languages.
- [Wikimedia Brief](https://github.com/sutton-signwriting/unicode-and-signwriting/blob/main/entries/wikimedia-brief/essay.md): Steve's account of the earlier Wikimedia technical work.

## The text model and preservation

- [Formal SignWriting](https://formal.signwriting.org/): specification of the working text model.
- [Formal SignWriting archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20074767): citable publication record.
- [Developer Notes](https://github.com/sutton-signwriting/unicode-and-signwriting/blob/main/entries/developer-notes/essay.md): FSW/SWU guidance and compatibility boundaries.
- [Facial Orthography Boundary](https://github.com/sutton-signwriting/unicode-and-signwriting/blob/main/entries/facial-orthography-boundary/essay.md): Steve's authored-data preservation assessment of the official Unicode facial model.

FSW and SWU preserve the same supported written-sign model through lossless
correspondence. SWU is separate from the official Unicode SignWriting design.
The compatibility assessment above is Steve's technical argument.

## Development and research

- [Sutton SignWriting projects](https://github.com/sutton-signwriting): core, rendering, component, and editor repositories.
- [SignMaker](https://github.com/sutton-signwriting/signmaker#readme): the editor, embedding and messaging documentation, and contributor credits.
- [SignWriting utilities](https://github.com/sign-language-processing/signwriting): structured data, conversion, tokenization, and rendering.
- [SignWriting evaluation](https://github.com/sign-language-processing/signwriting-evaluation): automatic comparisons of machine-generated writing.
- [SignBank+](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11566): multilingual translation-dataset preparation using large language models.
- [Publication library](https://steveslevinski.me/#section/publications): the released research record behind the discussion.

The workshop draws on the argument of Steve's forthcoming WLL article,
*Plane-Based Writing: Sutton SignWriting and the Organizational Axis in Writing-System
Typology*. The article asks what writing represents and how its written unit is organized.
